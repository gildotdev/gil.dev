# Customizing Casio Watches

A familiar digital watch can become a surprisingly personal object. The Casio clippings I saved on October 21, 2026 explore everything from a strap and a few visual details to replacing the electronics inside the case.

The thread connecting them: **pick a purpose or a visual theme, then choose modifications that support it.** A coherent watch can come from just a few deliberate changes.

## Three starting points

| Watch | Direction in the clippings | What makes it interesting |
|---|---|---|
| AE-1200, the “Casio Royale” | NASA theme; custom logo; half-negative display; oil filling | A face with several distinct areas to customize |
| W800 | SR-71-inspired Project Blackbird | A restrained palette, negative display, and custom strap working together |

These are examples from the saved builds, rather than a parts compatibility list. Exact case, module, strap, and adapter fit still need checking for an individual project.

## Build a theme before a parts list

### NASA Royale: make the strap part of the design

The AE-1200 shown by Adam Savage combines NASA graphics, blue lighting, and a distinctive strap suited to his orange ACES suit gloves. The result feels like a small piece of space equipment.

The useful lesson is how the face, light, and strap reinforce the same idea. The strap changes both the appearance and how the watch is worn; it deserves as much thought as the dial.

The transcript includes a parts list around **2:49–3:08**, but its brand names and LED specification are imperfectly transcribed. Check the video before turning that passage into an order.

### Project Blackbird: a small palette can carry the whole watch

Casi Mods’ W800 takes inspiration from the SR-71: black with red and yellow accents, a negative display, a custom Zulu leather strap, and case details.

The clipping’s description also mentions “enabling stopwatch.” Its transcript is mostly music and does not explain the modification, so that detail needs the full build write-up before treating it as a repeatable step.

Design takeaway: choose one reference object, extract its colors and markings, and apply them consistently.

## Change the face and display

Northern Lume Watches’ AE-1200 build combines three techniques: a printed logo, a half-negative display, and an oil-filled case.

- **Custom logo:** print onto transparency film and align it behind the faceplate. Placement matters as much as the artwork. See **1:31**.
- **Negative display:** replace the LCD’s front polarizing film and orient the replacement to produce the desired appearance. The build mixes positive and negative regions. See **3:06–5:19**.
- **Hydromod:** fill the case with oil, working to remove trapped bubbles before reassembly. The creator presents improved visibility as one motivation, especially with a negative display. See **5:25 onward**.

The practical detail worth remembering is the tiny piezo contact spring: the creator warns that losing it means losing the watch’s sounds. The LCD connectors and light spreader also need to return to their correct positions.

This is the most involved physical build in the collection. The creator calls it their hardest build and shows how fiddly assembly becomes under oil. The video also claims increased water resistance, but the clipping provides no pressure-test evidence or validated depth rating. Keep that claim separate from the demonstrated visual result.

## Change what the watch can do

Cam Shand’s video explores the **Ollee Watch** replacement module in a retro Casio. The saved demonstration includes Bluetooth time sync, step counting, interval timers, configurable alarms, RGB lighting, and games. Most settings can be changed on the watch; the creator says the companion app does not require an account.

This opens a different kind of customization: keeping the familiar case and segmented display while replacing the circuit board. The installation shown reuses the original battery terminals.

Useful limits from the clipping:

- The creator describes compatibility with module 593 watches, but also reports a counterfeit watch that did not work. Verify the actual donor and module before buying parts.
- The pulse feature calculates a rate from manually counted beats; it is not an optical heart-rate sensor.
- The creator found the temperature reading mixed body and ambient temperature, and disabled motion lighting after false triggers.
- The optional NTAG213 NFC tag is described as working with non-chrome-plated models.
- Tint can make the display too dark. Several improvised materials failed the creator’s readability test.
- The demonstrated 3D-printed housing loses water resistance.

The broader idea is appealing: upgrade an existing object while keeping its recognizable form and serviceable construction. Features and compatibility here describe the saved video, not a verified current product specification.

## A possible first project

My synthesis from these examples: start with a single theme and a strap change, then decide whether the face or electronics actually need work.

1. **Write a one-sentence brief.** For example: “An aviation-inspired daily watch with a dark case, restrained accents, and a readable display.”
2. **Choose the donor around the brief.** The Royale, Blackbird, and Ollee examples offer different directions.
3. **Try the exterior first.** Assess strap comfort, proportions, and color before opening the watch.
4. **Make one internal change at a time.** Check the display, buttons, light, and sound before adding another modification.
5. **Record the result.** Keep the exact donor model, parts, photos, readability observations, and any problems alongside the build.

## Questions to grow this note

- Which direction is most compelling: NASA equipment, Blackbird aviation, or a retro watch with modern functions?
- How does a negative or tinted display read in daylight and under its own backlight?
- Which modifications can be undone using the original parts?
- What does an Ollee module add that would be useful every day?
- Does the visual benefit of oil filling justify the mess and added servicing work?
- Could a custom face or printed housing connect this project with Wearable Cyberdeck?
